Movin on to more ball, Why is the Hemi, called Hemi? You ever wondered that? It's related to physics, because big Hemi V8s have an internal configuration that gives the combustion chamber a hemispherical shape, simple as that. Now why would that be an advantage. Regular engines have flat or wedge shaped combustion chambers, which leaves less space for the intake and exhaust valves, forcing to make em smaller, and since the sphere is the most efficient shape on surface area per volume ratio out of all the shapes The result is you can fit bigger intake and exhaust valves, which is the equivalent of fitting bigger lungs into your chest. Which means (mo power baby.gif) because more volume of air and fuel equals more explosion. Little lore moment, Dodge introduced this tech in 1951 with the 426 HEMI, and it won so much that NASCAR got angry and banned it. ALSO, modern HEMIs don't have full hemispherical chambers anymore because... (cough) emission standards, but they keep the name because it's a massive money making asset. But what's another thing modern HEMIs usually have? mmh, (superchager whine in background) Ah! Superchargers. Very quickly they're a pair of big screws placed like this inside of a box, the screws spin because they're tied by a belt to the engine's crankshaft, and as they spin they suck in air which the screws compress and force feed down the engine's throat. This makes the engine produce more power instantly, BUT at a disadvantage, since the belt it tied to the crankshaft it steals a little bit of power from the engine to get things moving, the bigger the supercharger the bigger the power loss, but the added power from running a supercharger drastically compensates it. (show visuals a la dmscales) A Turbochager on the other hand works by harvesting the exhaust flow, using it to spin a turbine, which spins another turbine, that sucks in fresh air, sending it through the intake and again force feeding the engine. But it too suffers from a problem. The infamous TURBO LAG, which basically happens because the exhaust flow is not strong enough (write -> usually at lower rpm) to spin the turbine which then has to spin the other turbine and- you get the deal. Basically the turbo can't force feed the engine until IT (the engine) is in a high enough rpm band. AND TO THAT ENGINEERS RESPONDED WITH ANTI-LAG, (zoom in on pops and bangs from your e-ray) Which works by keeping the throttle partially open even when you lift off the gas, that way some air enters and some combustion still happens, which generates some exhaust flow BUT when you need some aggressive flow, usually to spin up some massive turbo, the engine is tuned to retard ignition timings by a LOT, so that the explosion happens half where it needs to and half in the exhaust itself And IF YOU FOR SOME REASON NEED EVEN MORE THAN THAT, an added injection system sprays fuel directly in the exhaust, which makes explosions go off in it DIRECTLY, therefore creating a massive surge of exhaust flow. All of this keeps the turbo spooled up and ready for when you step on the throttle again, but you understand that it is pretty detrimental for the engine itself THAT's WHY they came up with an even better solution.
